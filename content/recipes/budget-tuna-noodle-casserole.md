## Why This One Works

Most versions of this casserole lean on a can of soup for the sauce, which costs real money and mostly tastes like salt. The sauce here is butter, flour and milk whisked together on the stove, and it comes out richer for a fraction of the price. Two cans of tuna spread across six servings works because the peas, the pasta and the sauce carry the dish, and cooking the pasta just short of done means it finishes in the oven soaking up sauce instead of going soft.

## Ingredients

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Heat the oven to 180 C (350 F). Cook the pasta in well salted water for about 2 minutes less than the package says. Drain it and set it aside.</li>
  <li>Melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion for about 3 minutes, until soft.</li>
  <li>Stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute. Pour in the milk a little at a time, whisking as you go, then simmer for about 3 minutes until the sauce thickens enough to coat a spoon. Salt and pepper it well.</li>
  <li>Take the pot off the heat. Fold in the drained pasta, the tuna broken into chunks, and the frozen peas.</li>
  <li>Scrape everything into a greased baking dish and scatter the grated cheese over the top.</li>
  <li>Bake for about 20 minutes, until the sauce bubbles at the edges and the cheese has melted into golden patches. Rest 5 minutes before serving.</li>
</ol>
