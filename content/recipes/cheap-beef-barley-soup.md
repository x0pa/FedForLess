## Why This One Works

Beef and barley soup usually means stewing beef, and stewing beef is where the budget goes to die. This version builds the pot on ground beef instead, the cheapest beef on the national average table, browned hard so the soup still tastes like it simmered all day. The barley does the heavy lifting in the bowl: it swells as it cooks, thickens the broth, and keeps a bowl filling for hours. The yield was measured, not guessed: about 4 litres of soup, eight bowls of roughly 500 ml each.

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the ground beef and the onion and cook for about 8 minutes, breaking the beef up with a spoon, until the beef is well browned and the onion has softened. Drain off any extra fat if the beef gave up a lot.</li>
  <li>Add the garlic, thyme and bay leaves and stir for 1 minute, until the garlic smells warm.</li>
  <li>Add the barley, the canned tomatoes, the bouillon cubes and about 2 litres of water. Bring to a boil.</li>
  <li>Stir in the potatoes, carrots and celery. Lower the heat and simmer, partially covered, for about 40 minutes, stirring now and then, until the barley is tender and chewy and the vegetables are soft.</li>
  <li>Fish out the bay leaves. Salt and pepper to taste, rest 5 minutes, and serve in deep bowls.</li>
</ol>
