## Why This One Works

Meatballs feel like a splurge until the pork is priced the way this site prices it: from a whole shoulder piece at $9.22 a kilogram instead of a small tray of ground meat at whatever the tray costs. The gravy is the second trick. It is not a can of anything and it is not cream; it is carrots and onion cooked soft and blended into a sweet, thick sauce that tastes far richer than its ingredients suggest, and it stretches half a bag of carrots across six plates. Baking the meatballs instead of frying them keeps hands free and fat low. The yield was measured, not guessed: 24 meatballs, four per person, over rice with gravy.

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Heat the oven to 200 C. Put the ground pork in a large bowl with the egg, the milk, the garlic, the paprika and a good pinch of salt and pepper. Mix with your hands until just combined, then roll the mix into 24 meatballs about the size of a walnut.</li>
  <li>Arrange the meatballs on a lined baking tray and bake for about 20 minutes, until browned and cooked through.</li>
  <li>Meanwhile heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and carrots and cook for about 8 minutes until softened. Pour in 600 ml of water and simmer for about 10 minutes, until the carrots are tender.</li>
  <li>Blend the carrot mixture until smooth, or mash it hard with a potato masher for a rustic gravy. Season to taste.</li>
  <li>Cook the rice. Spoon it into deep bowls, top each bowl with four meatballs, and ladle the carrot gravy over everything.</li>
</ol>
