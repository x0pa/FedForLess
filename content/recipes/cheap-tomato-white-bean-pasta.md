## Why This One Works

Pasta on its own is cheap but light; pasta with two cans of white beans stirred through is dinner. The beans add protein and fibre for $3.40 across the whole pot, and cooking the pasta in the sauce means the sauce costs nothing extra in water, energy or washing up. One can of tomatoes, one onion and a little cheese over the top carry the flavour, and the beans make a bowl of it hold you until morning. The yield was measured, not guessed: six deep bowls, each one a full serving with seconds in the pot for anyone who wants them.

## Ingredients

<ul class="ingredients nums">
  <li><span>Elbow macaroni, 450 g</span></li>
  <li><span>Canned white beans, two 540 ml cans</span></li>
  <li><span>Canned tomatoes, one 796 ml can</span></li>
  <li><span>Onions, 300 g, finely chopped</span></li>
  <li><span>Block cheese, 50 g, finely grated</span></li>
  <li><span>Canola oil, 2 tablespoons</span></li>
  <li><span>Garlic, 3 cloves, minced</span></li>
  <li><span>Dried oregano, 1 teaspoon</span></li>
  <li><span>Salt and pepper</span></li>
</ul>

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for about 6 minutes until soft and golden at the edges. Stir in the garlic and oregano for the last minute.</li>
  <li>Add the canned tomatoes, 500 ml of water and a good pinch of salt, and bring the pot to a boil.</li>
  <li>Stir in the pasta, lower the heat to a steady simmer, and cook for about 12 minutes, stirring often, until the pasta is tender and the sauce has thickened around it.</li>
  <li>Drain and rinse the beans and stir them through the pasta. Simmer for about 3 minutes more so the beans heat through and drink up some of the sauce.</li>
  <li>Rest the pot off the heat for 2 minutes, season to taste, and serve with the grated cheese scattered over each bowl.</li>
</ol>
