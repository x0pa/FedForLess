## Why This One Works

Frozen perogies are one of the great budget staples because the filling, the dough and most of the work are already done for a few dollars a pack. What they need is company. A pan of cabbage and onion cooked slowly in butter turns two plain packs into a dinner with sweetness, colour and real weight, and the sour cream on top does the classic job it always does beside this dish. The cabbage is doing the quiet budget work as usual: 800 g of it costs about two dollars and stretches the pan to six full plates. The yield was measured, not guessed: six servings of about 300 g of perogies each, with the cabbage and onion divided over the top.

  <li><span>Salt and pepper</span></li>

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Melt the butter in a large deep skillet over medium heat. Add the cabbage and the onion and cook for about 18 minutes, stirring now and then, until the cabbage has wilted down and softened and the onion has turned golden at the edges.</li>
  <li>While the cabbage cooks, bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add the perogies in two batches and cook until they float to the top, about 3 minutes, then cook for 1 minute more. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and let them drain well.</li>
  <li>Add the drained perogies to the skillet in one layer over the cabbage and onion. Cook for about 4 minutes, turning them once, until they are coated in the butter and golden in places.</li>
  <li>Scatter salt and pepper over the pan and toss everything gently so the cabbage and onion coat the perogies.</li>
  <li>Serve straight from the skillet in deep bowls, with a generous spoonful of sour cream on each bowl.</li>
</ol>
