## Why This One Works

Chili is usually a ground meat game, and ground meat prices show it. Pork shoulder plays by different rules: it is one of the cheapest cuts in the case, and a slow simmer turns it spoon tender while it flavours the whole pot. White beans keep the chili creamy and mild enough for everyone at the table, corn brings a little sweetness, and the beans stretch 700 g of pork across six bowls that nobody would call small.

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Pat the pork cubes dry and season them with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat and brown the pork well in two batches, about 5 minutes a batch. Set the pork aside.</li>
  <li>In the same pot, cook the onion for about 4 minutes, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom as it softens.</li>
  <li>Add the garlic, chili powder, cumin and paprika and stir for 1 minute, until the spices coat the onion and smell warm.</li>
  <li>Pour in the canned tomatoes and about 250 ml of water. Return the pork and any juices to the pot and bring it to a gentle simmer.</li>
  <li>Cover and simmer on low for about 25 minutes, stirring now and then.</li>
  <li>Stir in the beans and corn and simmer for about 10 minutes more, until the pork is tender and the chili has thickened. Salt to taste.</li>
  <li>Rest 5 minutes and serve in deep bowls. Leftovers keep about 3 months in the freezer.</li>
</ol>
