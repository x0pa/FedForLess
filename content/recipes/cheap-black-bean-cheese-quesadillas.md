## Why This One Works

Quesadillas earn their place on a budget site because everything in them works twice. The beans bring protein and fibre for the price of two cans, and mashing half of them turns the filling into something that holds together instead of rolling out of the tortilla. Block cheese grated at home melts better and costs less than a bag of shreds, and a dry pan, with no oil at all, is what keeps the tortillas crisp. This is the most expensive dinner in this batch of five posts and it is still a Tier 3 plate at under three dollars, which says something about what the other four are doing. The yield was measured, not guessed: eight large quesadillas, two per person.

## Method

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Drain and rinse the beans and put them in a large bowl. Mash about half of them roughly with a fork, leaving the rest whole.</li>
  <li>Stir the corn, the onion, the cumin and the chili powder through the beans, and season with salt and pepper.</li>
  <li>Lay the tortillas out and divide the filling over one half of each one. Scatter the grated cheese over the filling and fold the tortillas in half.</li>
  <li>Heat a dry large skillet over medium heat. Cook the quesadillas two at a time for about 3 minutes a side, pressing down lightly, until the tortillas are crisp and golden and the cheese has melted.</li>
  <li>Rest the quesadillas for 2 minutes, cut each one in half, and serve two per person with sour cream if you like.</li>
</ol>
