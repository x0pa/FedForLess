Cheap Chickpea Curry with Potato, Spinach and Rice

A cheap chickpea curry built on canned chickpeas, potato and frozen spinach in a spiced tomato sauce, spooned over rice and costed on Canadian average prices at about $1.95 a serving.

50 minTotal Time

Why This One Works

Curry at home often stalls at the coconut milk, because one can of it can cost as much as the chickpeas, the spinach and the onion put together. This version lets the tomato sauce do that work instead, with the spices bloomed in oil at the start so the whole pot tastes rounded and rich anyway. Chickpeas and potato make it filling in a way a vegetable curry often is not, the frozen spinach goes in straight from the bag with no washing and no wilting in batches, and rice underneath turns one pot into six full plates. The yield was measured, not guessed: about 2.4 litres of curry spooned over the rice, six plates of roughly 400 ml of curry each.

Ingredients Serves 6

  • Canned chickpeas, two 540 ml cans
  • Potatoes, 800 g, from the big bag
  • Frozen spinach, 300 g pack
  • Onions, 300 g
  • Canned tomatoes, 796 ml
  • White rice, 300 g
  • Canola oil, curry spices and garlic

Method

  1. Cook the rice in about 600 ml of salted water until tender, about 15 minutes. Fluff it and let it stand covered while the curry cooks.
  2. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for about 5 minutes, until it softens and starts to colour at the edges.
  3. Add the garlic, curry powder, cumin and turmeric and stir for 1 minute, until the spices coat the onion and smell warm.
  4. Add the potatoes, the canned tomatoes and about 250 ml of water. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer, partially covered, for about 12 minutes, until the potatoes are nearly tender.
  5. Stir in the chickpeas and simmer for about 5 minutes more, until the potatoes are fully tender and the sauce has thickened a little.
  6. Stir in the frozen spinach and cook for about 3 minutes, stirring now and then, until it has thawed and folded into the sauce. Salt to taste.
  7. Rest 5 minutes and serve the curry spooned over the rice in deep bowls.
Still Hungry?

Two spoonfuls of plain yogurt on top cool the spices and add about 20 cents a serving on average prices. The curry and rice are the meal on their own; the yogurt is for the top, and for anyone who likes the heat softened.

Questions

How much does this chickpea curry cost per serving?

About $1.95 a serving on Statistics Canada average retail prices for August 2026, for a pot of six servings costing $11.68 in ingredients. The basket cost, buying every package from an empty pantry, is $37.93. Depending on your province and store, a serving typically lands between $1.70 and $2.25, which keeps it in the Tier 2.

Can I use dried chickpeas instead of canned?

Yes. Dry beans and legumes sit at $3.58 for a 900 g bag on August 2026 averages, so about 250 g dry, soaked overnight and simmered until tender, lands near $1.00 and drops the cost by about 40 cents a serving. The canned version is priced here because it keeps the whole dish at 50 minutes with nothing to plan the night before.

Does this curry freeze well?

Very well, for about 3 months. Freeze the curry on its own and cook fresh rice when you serve it, and the plate tastes freshly made. The potatoes soften a little in the freezer, which suits the sauce, and the spinach holds up better frozen than it ever does in the fridge.
