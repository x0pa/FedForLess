Cheap Perogies with Cabbage, Onion and Sour Cream

One Pan · 45 Min · Tier 2 · Serves 6

A cheap perogies dinner built on frozen potato and cheddar perogies tossed with cabbage and onion cooked in butter and topped with sour cream, costed on Canadian prices at about $1.92 a serving.

Why This One Works

Frozen perogies are one of the great budget staples because the filling, the dough and most of the work are already done for a few dollars a pack. What they need is company. A pan of cabbage and onion cooked slowly in butter turns two plain packs into a dinner with sweetness, colour and real weight, and the sour cream on top does the classic job it always does beside this dish. The cabbage is doing the quiet budget work as usual: 800 g of it costs about two dollars and stretches the pan to six full plates. The yield was measured, not guessed: six servings of about 300 g of perogies each, with the cabbage and onion divided over the top.

Ingredients

  • Frozen perogies, two 907 g packsabout $5.98
  • Green cabbage, 800 g, thinly slicedabout $2.10
  • Onions, 300 g, thinly slicedabout $1.01
  • Sour cream, 250 mlabout $1.75
  • Butter, 30 gabout $0.38
  • Salt and pepperabout $0.30

Method

  1. Melt the butter in a large deep skillet over medium heat. Add the cabbage and the onion and cook for about 18 minutes, stirring now and then, until the cabbage has wilted down and softened and the onion has turned golden at the edges.
  2. While the cabbage cooks, bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add the perogies in two batches and cook until they float to the top, about 3 minutes, then cook for 1 minute more. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and let them drain well.
  3. Add the drained perogies to the skillet in one layer over the cabbage and onion. Cook for about 4 minutes, turning them once, until they are coated in the butter and golden in places.
  4. Scatter salt and pepper over the pan and toss everything gently so the cabbage and onion coat the perogies.
  5. Serve straight from the skillet in deep bowls, with a generous spoonful of sour cream on each bowl.
Still Hungry?

A fried egg on top adds about 40 cents a serving on average prices and turns the plate into a full brunch as happily as a dinner. The skillet is the meal on its own; the egg is for slow days, and for anyone who wants the sour cream to meet a runny yolk.

Questions

How much do these perogies cost per serving?

About $1.92 a serving, for a skillet of six servings costing $11.52 in ingredients. The cabbage, onions and butter are priced on Statistics Canada average retail prices for August 2026, and the perogies and sour cream are priced from chain catalogue checks in October 2026 because the national table does not list them. The basket cost, buying every package from an empty pantry, is $21.83. Depending on your province and store, a serving typically lands between $1.70 and $2.15, which puts it in Tier 2.

Do the perogies need to boil before they go into the pan?

Yes. Frozen perogies need the boiling water first so the dough cooks through and the filling heats all the way to the centre. The short turn in the hot pan afterwards is for colour and for the butter, and it only works well if the perogies arrive in the pan already tender and well drained.

How filling is a serving, and how do leftovers keep?

Each serving is about 300 g of perogies under a blanket of cabbage and onion with sour cream on top, at an estimate of around 640 calories with about 17 g of protein. Leftovers keep for up to 3 days in the fridge and reheat best in a covered skillet over low heat with a splash of water, which brings the cabbage back to life better than a microwave does.
