Cheap Lentil and Potato Curry

A cheap lentil and potato curry built on red lentils, potatoes and canned tomato over rice, costed on Canadian prices at about $1.27 a serving and filling enough to end the day properly.

Why This One Works

Red lentils are one of the cheapest real proteins in the store, and red lentils in particular ask for almost nothing: no soaking, no long simmer, no separate cooking. They fall apart into the sauce and thicken it as they go, so a pot built on lentils, potatoes and one can of tomatoes eats like something that simmered all afternoon. The potatoes do the stretching work, the rice underneath makes it a full plate, and the whole pot of six servings costs less than a single takeout sandwich. The yield was measured, not guessed: six deep bowls of curry over rice, each one a full meal.

Ingredients Serves 6

  • Dried red lentils, 400 g
  • Potatoes, 800 g, cut into bite size pieces
  • Onion, 300 g, finely chopped
  • Canned tomatoes, one 796 ml can
  • White rice, 300 g
  • Canola oil, 2 tablespoons
  • Curry powder, 2 tablespoons
  • Ground cumin, 1 teaspoon
  • Ground turmeric, 1 teaspoon
  • Garlic, 3 cloves, minced
  • Salt

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for about 5 minutes until softened at the edges.
  2. Stir in the curry powder, cumin, turmeric and garlic and cook for about 1 minute, until the pot smells warm and spiced.
  3. Add the lentils, potatoes, canned tomatoes and 900 ml of water. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for about 25 minutes, stirring now and then, until the lentils have broken down and the potatoes are tender.
  4. Meanwhile cook the rice in a second pot according to the pack. Let it stand covered for 5 minutes, then fluff it with a fork.
  5. Season the curry with salt, simmer for 2 minutes more, and serve it over the rice in deep bowls.
Still Hungry?

A fried egg on top adds about 40 cents a serving on average prices and sits happily in the curry sauce. The pot is the meal on its own; the egg is for the days when dinner needs to feel like more of an event.

Questions

How much does this lentil and potato curry cost per serving?

About $1.27 a serving on Statistics Canada average retail prices for August 2026, for a pot of six servings costing $7.64 in ingredients. The basket cost, buying every package from an empty pantry, is $35.91, and most of that is the bags of lentils, potatoes and rice you will keep cooking from for weeks. Depending on your province and store, a serving typically lands between $1.13 and $1.43, which puts it in Tier 1.

Do red lentils need to be soaked before cooking?

No. Red lentils are the fast member of the lentil family: they go straight into the pot dry and break down in about 25 minutes of simmering, which is exactly what gives this curry its thick body. Give them a rinse first and pick out any small stones, and that is all the prep they ask for.

How filling is a serving, and how do leftovers keep?

Very. Each serving pairs a deep bowl of curry with rice, at an estimate of around 520 calories with about 19 g of protein and 14 g of fibre, much of it from the lentils themselves. Leftovers keep for up to 4 days in the fridge and freeze well for up to 3 months. The curry thickens as it sits, so loosen it with a splash of water when you reheat it.
