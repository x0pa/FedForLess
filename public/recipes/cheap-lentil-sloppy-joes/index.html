Cheap Lentil Sloppy Joes

Cheap lentil sloppy joes built on dried lentils simmered in canned tomatoes with onion, carrot and warm spices, spooned onto soft buns and costed on Canadian prices at about $1.43 a serving.

Why This One Works

Sloppy joes were always meant to be generous food for very little money, and lentils suit the job even better than meat does. They cook right in the tomato sauce, soak up the warm spices, and turn tender and saucy with no browning and no draining. The onion and carrot melt into the filling and add a quiet sweetness, the mustard and vinegar keep the rich sauce bright, and a little brown sugar rounds it all out the way a classic sloppy joe should taste. The yield was measured as six buns piled high, each one filling enough to be the meal on its own. The filling tastes even better the next day, and it freezes well for a night when cooking needs to be nothing more than warming a pot and opening a pack of buns.

Ingredients Serves 6

  • Dried brown lentils, 400 g, rinsed and picked over
  • Diced tomatoes, 1 can (796 ml)
  • Onion, 300 g, finely chopped
  • Carrots, 200 g, finely chopped
  • Hamburger buns, 6, from a pack of 8
  • Canola oil, 2 tablespoons
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, minced
  • Chili powder, 2 tablespoons
  • Brown sugar, 2 tablespoons
  • Yellow mustard, 1 tablespoon
  • White vinegar, 1 tablespoon
  • Smoked paprika, 1 teaspoon
  • Salt, 1 teaspoon
  • Black pepper, 1/2 teaspoon

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and the carrot and cook for about 6 minutes, stirring now and then, until they have softened.
  2. Add the garlic, the chili powder and the smoked paprika. Cook for about 1 minute, stirring all the time, until the spices smell warm and toasty.
  3. Add the lentils and the canned tomatoes with all their juice. Pour in 750 ml of water, stir well, and bring the pot to a gentle boil.
  4. Lower the heat, cover the pot, and simmer for about 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the lentils are tender and the sauce has thickened.
  5. Stir in the brown sugar, the mustard, the vinegar, the salt and the pepper. Simmer uncovered for about 5 minutes more, until the filling is thick enough to pile on a bun, then taste it and add a little more salt if it needs it.
  6. Spoon the hot filling generously onto the buns and serve at once while everything is steaming.
Still Hungry?

A handful of frozen corn stirred into the filling for the last few minutes adds about 25 cents a serving on average prices and brings a little sweetness and colour to every bite. The sandwich is the meal on its own; the corn is for anyone who likes their sloppy joes with a bit more going on inside.

Questions

How much do these lentil sloppy joes cost per serving?

About $1.43 a serving, for a batch of six sandwiches costing $8.55 in ingredients on Statistics Canada average retail prices for August 2026, with the buns priced from a chain catalogue check in October 2026 because the national table does not list them. The basket cost, buying every package from an empty pantry, is $28.69. Depending on your province and store, a serving typically lands between $1.20 and $1.65, which puts it in Tier 1.

Does the filling freeze well?

Yes. Cool the filling fully, pack it in flat containers with a little room at the top, and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw in the fridge overnight and warm it gently on the stove with a splash of water, stirring now and then, until it is steaming all the way through. Spoon it onto fresh buns when you serve it.

How filling is a serving?

Each serving is a bun piled high with saucy lentils, onion and carrot, at an estimate of around 480 calories with about 24 g of protein and about 12 g of fibre. The lentils do the filling work, so one sandwich stands on its own as the meal with nothing needed beside it.
