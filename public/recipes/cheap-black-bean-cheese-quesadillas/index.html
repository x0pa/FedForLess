Cheap Black Bean and Cheese Quesadillas

A cheap quesadilla dinner built on canned black beans, corn, onion and block cheese in big flour tortillas, costed on Canadian prices at about $2.91 a serving for two crisp quesadillas each.

Why This One Works

Quesadillas earn their place on a budget site because everything in them works twice. The beans bring protein and fibre for the price of two cans, and mashing half of them turns the filling into something that holds together instead of rolling out of the tortilla. Block cheese grated at home melts better and costs less than a bag of shreds, and a dry pan, with no oil at all, is what keeps the tortillas crisp. This is the most expensive dinner in this batch of five posts and it is still a Tier 3 plate at under three dollars, which says something about what the other four are doing. The yield was measured, not guessed: eight large quesadillas, two per person.

Ingredients Serves 4

  • Flour tortillas, one 650 g pack of 8 large
  • Canned black beans, two 540 ml cans
  • Block cheese, 200 g, grated
  • Frozen corn, 150 g
  • Onion, 150 g, finely chopped
  • Cumin and chili powder
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Drain and rinse the beans and put them in a large bowl. Mash about half of them roughly with a fork, leaving the rest whole.
  2. Stir the corn, the onion, the cumin and the chili powder through the beans, and season with salt and pepper.
  3. Lay the tortillas out and divide the filling over one half of each one. Scatter the grated cheese over the filling and fold the tortillas in half.
  4. Heat a dry large skillet over medium heat. Cook the quesadillas two at a time for about 3 minutes a side, pressing down lightly, until the tortillas are crisp and golden and the cheese has melted.
  5. Rest the quesadillas for 2 minutes, cut each one in half, and serve two per person with sour cream if you like.
Still Hungry?

A quick cabbage slaw on the side adds crunch and a second vegetable for about 30 cents a serving on average prices. The quesadillas are the meal on their own; the slaw is for the nights when the table wants something fresh beside them.

Questions

How much do these quesadillas cost per serving?

About $2.91 a serving on Statistics Canada average retail prices for August 2026, with the tortillas priced from chain catalogue checks, for a batch of four servings costing $11.62 in ingredients. That puts them in Tier 3, the top band on this site, and they are printed anyway because two big crisp quesadillas with beans and cheese is a real dinner. The basket cost, buying every package from an empty pantry, is $22.92. Depending on your province and store, a serving typically lands between $2.60 and $3.20.

Can quesadillas be made ahead and frozen?

Yes, and they freeze better uncooked. Assemble the quesadillas, stack them with a piece of baking paper between each one, and freeze them flat in a bag for up to 2 months. Cook them from frozen in a dry pan over medium low heat, giving them a few extra minutes a side so the centre heats through.

How filling is a serving, and how do leftovers keep?

A serving is two large quesadillas, at an estimate of around 780 calories with about 31 g of protein, with the beans and cheese doing the heavy lifting. Cooked leftovers keep for up to 3 days in the fridge and come back best in a dry skillet, which crisps the tortilla again; a microwave softens them.
