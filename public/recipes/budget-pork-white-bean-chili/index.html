Budget Pork and White Bean Chili

A budget pork and white bean chili built on pork shoulder, the cut that stays cheap and turns tender, costed on Canadian average prices and made to fill six deep bowls at about $2.45 a serving.

Why This One Works

Chili is usually a ground meat game, and ground meat prices show it. Pork shoulder plays by different rules: it is one of the cheapest cuts in the case, and a slow simmer turns it spoon tender while it flavours the whole pot. White beans keep the chili creamy and mild enough for everyone at the table, corn brings a little sweetness, and the beans stretch 700 g of pork across six bowls that nobody would call small.

Ingredients Serves 6

  • Pork shoulder, 700 g, cubed
  • Canned white beans, two 540 ml cans
  • Canned tomatoes, 796 ml
  • Frozen corn, 300 g
  • Onion, 200 g
  • Canola oil, 15 ml
  • Chili powder, 2 tablespoons
  • Ground cumin, 2 teaspoons
  • Paprika, 1 teaspoon
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, minced

Method

  1. Pat the pork cubes dry and season them with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat and brown the pork well in two batches, about 5 minutes a batch. Set the pork aside.
  2. In the same pot, cook the onion for about 4 minutes, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom as it softens.
  3. Add the garlic, chili powder, cumin and paprika and stir for 1 minute, until the spices coat the onion and smell warm.
  4. Pour in the canned tomatoes and about 250 ml of water. Return the pork and any juices to the pot and bring it to a gentle simmer.
  5. Cover and simmer on low for about 25 minutes, stirring now and then.
  6. Stir in the beans and corn and simmer for about 10 minutes more, until the pork is tender and the chili has thickened. Salt to taste.
  7. Rest 5 minutes and serve in deep bowls. Leftovers keep about 3 months in the freezer.
Still Hungry?

A scoop of plain rice under the chili stretches the pot even further, about 20 cents a serving on average prices, and a spoonful of sour cream on top adds about 15 cents.

Questions

How much does this chili cost per serving?

About $2.45 a serving on Canadian average prices checked for August 2026. The full pot costs $14.67 in ingredients and serves six generous bowls, with most regions landing between $2.15 and $2.85 a serving depending on the price of pork shoulder.

Why pork shoulder instead of ground pork?

Pork shoulder is one of the cheapest cuts per kilogram and it turns tender and rich during the simmer, where lean cuts would go dry. Cubed shoulder also gives the chili real texture, more like a stew than a sauce.

Does this chili freeze well?

Very well, for about 3 months. Cool it fully, pack it flat in freezer bags or in portions, and reheat from frozen on the stove with a splash of water. The beans soften a little more and the flavour deepens.
