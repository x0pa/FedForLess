Cheap Pork Meatballs with Rice

A cheap meatball dinner built on ground pork priced from a shoulder piece, baked and served in a simple carrot and onion gravy over rice, costed on Canadian prices at about $1.98 a serving.

Why This One Works

Meatballs feel like a splurge until the pork is priced the way this site prices it: from a whole shoulder piece at $9.22 a kilogram instead of a small tray of ground meat at whatever the tray costs. The gravy is the second trick. It is not a can of anything and it is not cream; it is carrots and onion cooked soft and blended into a sweet, thick sauce that tastes far richer than its ingredients suggest, and it stretches half a bag of carrots across six plates. Baking the meatballs instead of frying them keeps hands free and fat low. The yield was measured, not guessed: 24 meatballs, four per person, over rice with gravy.

Ingredients Serves 6

  • Ground pork, 700 g
  • Long grain rice, 400 g
  • Carrots, 500 g, sliced
  • Onion, 300 g, finely chopped
  • Egg, 1
  • Milk, 100 ml
  • Canola oil, 1 tablespoon
  • Garlic, 2 cloves, minced
  • Paprika, 1 teaspoon
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 C. Put the ground pork in a large bowl with the egg, the milk, the garlic, the paprika and a good pinch of salt and pepper. Mix with your hands until just combined, then roll the mix into 24 meatballs about the size of a walnut.
  2. Arrange the meatballs on a lined baking tray and bake for about 20 minutes, until browned and cooked through.
  3. Meanwhile heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and carrots and cook for about 8 minutes until softened. Pour in 600 ml of water and simmer for about 10 minutes, until the carrots are tender.
  4. Blend the carrot mixture until smooth, or mash it hard with a potato masher for a rustic gravy. Season to taste.
  5. Cook the rice. Spoon it into deep bowls, top each bowl with four meatballs, and ladle the carrot gravy over everything.
Still Hungry?

A pan of cabbage fried in a little butter on the side costs about 40 cents a serving on average prices and belongs beside meatballs and gravy. The bowls are the meal on their own; the cabbage is for the nights when the table wants a second pan in the middle.

Questions

How much do these meatballs cost per serving?

About $1.98 a serving on Statistics Canada average retail prices for August 2026, for a batch of six servings costing $11.88 in ingredients. The pork is priced from a whole pork shoulder piece at $9.22 a kilogram, the way this site prices all its ground pork, rather than at tray pack prices. The basket cost, buying every package from an empty pantry, is $49.24, and most of that is the bags of rice, carrots and onions and the bottle of oil you will keep using. Depending on your province and store, a serving typically lands between $1.76 and $2.22, which puts it in Tier 2.

Can the meatballs be frozen?

Yes, cooked or raw. Freeze cooked meatballs in their gravy in portions for up to 3 months and reheat them gently on the stove. Raw meatballs freeze well on a tray first, then in a bag, also for up to 3 months; thaw them in the fridge overnight before baking.

How filling is a serving, and how do leftovers keep?

A serving is four meatballs with rice and gravy, at an estimate of around 610 calories with about 27 g of protein. It is a full plate by any measure. Leftovers keep for up to 3 days in the fridge, and the gravy loosens again with a splash of water when you reheat it.
