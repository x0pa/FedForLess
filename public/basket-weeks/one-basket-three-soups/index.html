Cheap Weekly Meal Plan: One Basket, Three Soups

Three of the cheapest soups on this site were quietly built on the same bags. Buy the basket once and the week mostly cooks itself: 22 servings from one $58.35 shop.

One combined basket$58.35
The three baskets bought separately$108.88
You save$50.53
Serves 22 · $2.65 a serving at the till

Every recipe on Fed For Less prints two prices: what the food in the pot costs, and what the basket costs at the till. The basket is the number that stings. A soup can cost $1.34 a serving to eat and still ask for a $30 shop before you start. This plan answers that problem head on. Three soups, planned together, share one basket: the same bag of potatoes, the same bag of carrots, the same bag of onions, one bottle of oil. Buy it once and the week is mostly cooked.

The one shop

One pass through the store buys everything below. Prices are the same Canadian average prices the three recipes were costed on, checked in August 2026.

  • Ground beef, 500 g: $8.38, bought by weight at $16.75 a kilogram
  • Ham from pork shoulder, one piece of about 900 g: $8.30 at $9.22 a kilogram; 500 g goes in the pot and the rest keeps for another meal
  • Pearl barley, one 450 g bag: $2.49
  • Dried red lentils, one 900 g bag: $3.63
  • Dry split peas, one 900 g bag: $3.58
  • Potatoes, one 4.54 kg bag: $4.87
  • Carrots, one 1.36 kg bag: $3.89
  • Onions, one 1.36 kg bag: $4.58
  • Celery, one unit: $2.68
  • Canned tomatoes, two 796 ml cans: $4.36
  • Canola oil, one 3 litre bottle: $10.51
  • Pantry allowance for garlic, herbs, bouillon, salt and pepper across all three pots: $1.08

The whole shop comes to $58.35. Bought as three separate baskets, the same food costs $108.88.

The three pots

Cheap Beef and Barley Soup

The big one. Ground beef and pearl barley in a tomato broth with potato, carrot and celery. The batch costs $16.26 and fills eight deep bowls, about $2.03 a serving. Cook this one first, while the celery is at its best.

Cheap Lentil Soup with Potato and Carrot

The cheapest pot on the site. Red lentils need no soaking and cook fast, so this is the weeknight pot. The batch costs $8.02 and serves six, about $1.34 a serving.

Cheap Split Pea and Ham Soup

The slow weekend pot. Split peas and ham simmer down into something thick enough to stand a spoon in. The batch costs $9.67 and serves eight, about $1.21 a serving.

Together the three pots hold 22 servings, and the food in them costs $33.95 all in, about $1.54 a serving.

A week of eating

For two adults, 22 servings is a week of dinners with bowls left over for lunches. Cook the beef and barley soup on day one and eat it again on day three. The lentil soup lands mid week, when nobody wants a project. The split pea soup suits the weekend, when a pot can simmer all afternoon. Keep cooked soup in the fridge for up to four days, and freeze the rest in portions on the day you cook it. Every pot on this plan freezes well.

What is left for next week

  • About 2.5 kg of the potato bag
  • 450 g of the lentil bag and 450 g of the split pea bag, plus 150 g of barley
  • About 560 g of onions and a little of the carrot bag
  • Half the celery and nearly the whole bottle of oil
  • About 400 g of the ham piece

None of it is waste. The dry goods keep for months, the oil keeps longer, and next week starts with a head start that is already paid for.

The math, honestly

The three baskets on the recipe pages add up to $108.88: $39.99 plus $30.07 plus $38.82. One shared shop is $58.35. The saving is $50.53, close to half, and it comes from a boring place: bags and bottles you only buy once.

Two numbers describe this week. At the till, the week costs $2.65 a serving, because the till is where the basket lands. On the plate, the food costs $1.54 a serving, because much of the basket is still in the cupboard waiting for next week. Both numbers are printed because both are true.

If your store charges more or less than the national averages, the shape of the saving holds: overlap is overlap. The Recipe Cost Calculator will rerun any of the three pots at your own prices.

Questions

Do I have to cook all three soups in the same week?

No. The bags of lentils, split peas and barley keep for months, and the potatoes, carrots and onions hold for weeks in a cool cupboard. The ham and the celery set the pace: cook the split pea and beef soups inside the first week, or freeze the ham for later. The pair box on each recipe page shows the two pot version of this plan if three pots feels like too much soup.

What if nobody wants soup three times in a week?

Freeze two of the pots in portions and spread them across the month. The basket math does not change, and a frozen bowl of split pea soup on a tired Tuesday is still a $1.21 dinner.

Can I swap one of the soups for something else?

Yes, if the swap shares the bags. The pair box on each recipe page lists its partners, and more pairings land as new recipes publish. Any recipe that shares the potato, carrot and onion bags keeps most of the saving.
